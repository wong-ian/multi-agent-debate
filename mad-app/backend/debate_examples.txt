BELOW ARE EXAMPLES OF ACTUAL FAILURES IN A DEBATE CONTEXT.
USE THESE TO DISTINGUISH BETWEEN "HEALTHY DEBATE" AND "SYSTEM FAILURE".

######################################
### FAILURE EXAMPLE: 1.3 Step Repetition (Looping) ###
Description: The agent adds ZERO new information.
Round 1:
Debater A: We must ban plastic straws because they harm 1 million sea birds annually.

Round 2:
Debater B: Actually, fishing nets are 46% of ocean plastic. Straws are negligible.
Debater A: We must ban plastic straws because they harm 1 million sea birds annually.

ANALYSIS:
YES (1.3). Debater A repeated the Round 1 argument verbatim, completely ignoring the new counter-evidence about fishing nets.

######################################
### SUCCESS EXAMPLE: 1.3 Step Repetition (Reinforcement) ###
Description: The agent keeps their stance but adds NEW information. This is NOT a failure.
Round 1:
Debater A: We must ban plastic straws to save marine life.

Round 2:
Debater B: Straws are only 0.02% of waste.
Debater A: While the percentage is small, banning straws is a "gateway policy" that shifts consumer habits toward broader conservation efforts.

ANALYSIS:
NO FAILURE. Debater A maintained their stance ("Ban Straws") but added NEW reasoning ("gateway policy"). This is valid debate progress.

######################################
### FAILURE EXAMPLE: 2.3 Task Derailment ###
Description: The agent completely leaves the domain of the debate.
Topic: "Is Nuclear Energy Safe?"

Round 1:
Debater A: Nuclear energy provides base-load power with zero carbon emissions.

Round 2:
Debater B: Speaking of emissions, I really love the new exhaust sound on the Ford Mustang. Do you like muscle cars?

ANALYSIS:
YES (2.3). The agent switched from "Nuclear Energy" to "Muscle Cars." This is a total derailment.

######################################
### SUCCESS EXAMPLE: 2.3 Task Derailment (Sub-topic) ###
Description: The agent discusses a related philosophical concept. This is NOT a failure.
Topic: "Is Nuclear Energy Safe?"

Round 1:
Debater A: Nuclear energy is safe.

Round 2:
Debater B: Safety isn't just physical; it's ethical. We are leaving waste for 10,000 years. Is it "safe" to burden future generations?

ANALYSIS:
NO FAILURE. Discussing "Ethics" and "Future Generations" is relevant to the topic of "Safety."

######################################
### FAILURE EXAMPLE: 2.5 Ignored Other Agent's Input ###
Description: The agent ignores a direct question.
Round 1:
Debater A: UBI causes inflation.
Debater B: Studies show it doesn't. Can you cite a specific study that proves your inflation claim?

Round 2:
Debater A: Also, UBI makes people lazy. I think work gives life meaning.

ANALYSIS:
YES (2.5). Debater A completely ignored the direct request for a citation and moved to a new topic ("Laziness").

######################################
### SUCCESS EXAMPLE: 2.5 Ignored Other Agent's Input (Disagreement) ###
Description: The agent refuses to concede but addresses the point. This is NOT a failure.
Round 1:
Debater B: Can you cite a study proving inflation?

Round 2:
Debater A: I don't need a specific study; basic economic theory dictates that increasing money supply drives up prices.

ANALYSIS:
NO FAILURE. Debater A addressed the question ("I don't need a study") even though they refused to provide one. They did not ignore the prompt.
